\begin{abstract}
Learning from temporal data requires a representation that retains useful history while controlling statistical and computational complexity. We introduce \textsc{QuaRK}, a quantum-reservoir-based temporal learning framework that combines a finite-set Johnson--Lindenstrauss projection, independently parameterized reset-contractive quantum reservoirs, local-observable features, and a regularized Mat\'ern kernel readout. The analysis separates four properties that are sometimes conflated in quantum reservoir learning. First, the reset channel yields exact trace-norm contraction and a geometrically fading dependence on initialization. Second, distinct exact feature vectors can be interpolated by a finite-norm Mat\'ern RKHS readout, a conditional capacity statement that does not assert injectivity of the quantum feature map. Third, local Pauli observables admit a classical-shadow measurement guarantee whose finite-shot error can be propagated to a fixed readout. Fourth, for a fixed exact featurizer and stationary beta-mixing input--output data, an independent-block argument gives a one-sided risk bound with an explicit effective block count and a finite-window truncation term. These results provide a resource-aware and assumption-explicit foundation for evaluating quantum reservoir kernels without asserting quantum advantage, hardware-noise robustness, or superiority to classical temporal models.
\end{abstract}
