\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented \textsc{QuaRK}, a resource-aware temporal-learning framework
that combines reset-contractive quantum reservoirs, concatenated
local-observable features, and a regularized Mat\'ern kernel readout. The
analysis establishes exact trace-norm contraction, geometrically decaying
finite-window feature error, stability of RKHS predictions to feature
perturbations, and conditional interpolation of distinct finite feature
sets. For a fixed exact featurizer and stationary beta-mixing observations,
the corrected independent-block analysis gives a one-sided true-risk bound
whose stochastic terms depend on an effective block count and whose
approximation term exposes the reservoir memory scale. A separate
classical-shadow result accounts for finite-shot estimation of local Pauli
features.

These statements provide a rigorous foundation for evaluating the method,
not evidence of quantum advantage or classical superiority. The remaining
submission work is empirical: the completed experiment artifacts must be
integrated under matched protocols, with uncertainty and resource accounting,
before claims about competitiveness, operating regimes, or practical
plausibility can be assessed.
