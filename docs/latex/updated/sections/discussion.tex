\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What is specific to the quantum reservoir.}
The state update, its reset-channel implementation, and the observable
measurement process are quantum. The statistical readout and the JL
preprocessing are classical. The theory proves useful structural properties
of this complete construction, but it does not isolate a predictive benefit
that cannot be reproduced by a classical nonlinear reservoir or random
feature map. The appropriate comparative question is therefore empirical and
must use matched feature, readout, split, and model-selection protocols.

\paragraph{Fixed-design learning theory.}
\Cref{thm:generalization} is uniform over an RKHS norm ball after the
projection, reservoirs, observable family, window scheme, and kernel
hyperparameters have been fixed independently of the analyzed sample. If
these choices are selected on training or validation data, the theorem must
be combined with a separate model-selection argument. Bounded labels and
stationary beta mixing are substantive assumptions; nonstationarity,
heavy-tailed outcomes, and online adaptation are outside the current result.

\paragraph{Effective sample size.}
The independent-block parameter \(a\) is not a cosmetic proof variable.
Increasing \(a\) weakens the coupling penalty through
\(\beta_Z(as-w)\) but decreases the effective block count
\(\mu=N/(2a)\). Consequently, the theory does not predict a universal
\(1/\sqrt N\) curve for a fixed gap. Under geometric mixing, the explicit
block schedule in \cref{cor:geometric-mixing} yields a logarithmic degradation
from the independent rate.

\paragraph{Window approximation.}
Contraction produces a transparent finite-window error, but it also imposes a
memory trade-off. Stronger contraction decreases the worst-case effect of the
remote past; it may simultaneously suppress task-relevant history. The bound
does not select \(w\) or \(\lambda_r\), nor does it imply monotonic predictive
improvement as either parameter changes.

\paragraph{Measurement and hardware scope.}
The generalization theorem uses exact expectations. The shadow result covers
finite sampling under a particular randomized local-Pauli measurement model,
and \cref{cor:measurement-stability} concerns evaluation of a fixed readout.
Neither result covers training instability from noisy Gram matrices, gate
noise, reset error, decoherence, calibration drift, or correlated device
measurements. The reset circuit in \cref{fig:reset-dilation} is an
implementable dilation, not real-device validation.

\paragraph{Resource interpretation.}
The JL statement controls the logical encoded width for a finite point set;
it does not remove the input-dimension dependence of classical projection.
Sequential spatial reuse can keep peak quantum width at one reservoir plus
reset ancillas, but it increases the number of executions. Local shadows
reduce the dependence on the number of requested observables to a logarithm
for fixed locality while retaining a \(3^k\) dependence on locality. Finally,
dense kernel ridge regression has quadratic memory and cubic training time
unless an additional approximation or solver is introduced.

\paragraph{No advantage claim.}
Nothing in the interpolation or generalization result distinguishes quantum
features from arbitrary fixed features satisfying the same assumptions.
Accordingly, this work does not claim quantum advantage, asymptotic runtime
speedup, state-of-the-art accuracy, or general superiority over classical
temporal models. Its practical case must rest on transparent operating-point
comparisons after the empirical section is completed.

\paragraph{Societal impact.}
The present work develops a general-purpose learning method and does not
introduce a domain-specific deployment. Its principal foreseeable risks are
the ordinary risks of time-series prediction---unreliable extrapolation,
distribution shift, and overconfidence---together with inflated resource or
advantage claims in an early-stage quantum setting. The explicit assumptions
and limitations are intended to reduce those risks.
