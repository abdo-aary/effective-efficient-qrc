\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related-work}

\paragraph{Reservoir computing and fading memory.}
Echo-state networks and liquid-state machines introduced the principle of
driving a fixed recurrent system and training only its readout
\citep{Jaeger2001,MaassNatschlagerMarkram2002}. Fading memory formalizes
continuity of a causal filter with respect to increasingly remote inputs
\citep{BoydChua1985}. Contractive state updates provide a direct sufficient
condition for the echo-state and fading-memory properties, while universality
and risk analyses study different questions: the functions representable by
families of reservoirs and the statistical behavior of their learned
readouts, respectively \citep{GrigoryevaOrtega2018,GononGrigoryevaOrtega2020}.
Our contraction result concerns one fixed reset-based architecture and is not
a universality theorem.

\paragraph{Quantum reservoir computing.}
Quantum reservoir computing uses quantum dynamics as the driven state system
\citep{FujiiNakajima2017}. Existing proposals study dissipative dynamics,
reset mechanisms, noisy processors, weak or repeated measurements, and
spatial multiplexing
\citep{ChenNurdin2019,ChenNurdinYamamoto2020,NakajimaEtAl2019,
MolteniDestriPrati2023,MujalEtAl2023,HuEtAl2024}. Finite-dimensional quantum
reservoir filters and the role of input dependence have also received formal
treatment \citep{MartinezPenaOrtega2023}. \textsc{QuaRK} uses a reset channel
because its contraction coefficient is exact and directly tunable. Multiple
reservoir instances enlarge the classical observable vector, but they may be
executed sequentially on reused hardware; consequently, the instance count is
not automatically a peak-width requirement.

\paragraph{Quantum features and kernel readouts.}
Quantum feature methods encode classical data into states or measurement
statistics before applying a classical learner
\citep{SchuldKilloran2019,HuangBroughtonEtAl2021}. \textsc{QuaRK} belongs to
this broad family, with the important difference that its features are
generated dynamically from a temporal window rather than independently from
one static input. The Mat\'ern readout is an ordinary classical positive
definite kernel on the measured feature vectors. Its role here is both
algorithmic---kernel ridge regression provides a regularized nonlinear
readout---and analytic---the normalized kernel bounds predictions in an RKHS
ball and its spectral second moment controls sensitivity to feature
perturbations \citep{Genton2001,PorcuEtAl2024,HofmannScholkopfSmola2008}.
Calling the complete pipeline quantum-reservoir-based does not imply that its
kernel has no comparable classical construction.

\paragraph{Random projection and measurement.}
The JL lemma preserves the geometry of a finite point set under a random
linear projection whose target dimension depends logarithmically on the set
size \citep{DasguptaGupta2003,Woodruff2014}. We use this result to account for
the width needed to encode the observed finite set. It leaves the classical
cost of multiplying by the projection matrix and the subsequent dynamics
untouched. For measurement, classical shadows can estimate many observables
from shared randomized measurements \citep{HuangKuengPreskill2020}. Under
local random Pauli measurements, a Pauli string of weight at most \(k\) has a
shadow-norm-squared scaling of \(3^k\); this is the factor used in our resource
statement.

\paragraph{Learning with dependent observations.}
Independent-block arguments compare a beta-mixing sequence to independent
blocks and yield uniform learning bounds in terms of an effective block count
\citep{Yu1994,MohriRostamizadeh2008,DedeckerEtAl2007}. Our result applies this
machinery to a process of strided temporal windows. It differs from an
i.i.d.\ bound in two linked ways: the confidence condition contains the
mixing coefficient at the block separation, and the stochastic terms scale
with the number of blocks rather than the raw number of windows. The analysis
is conditional on a fixed featurizer and does not cover data-dependent
selection among reservoir or kernel configurations.
