\begin{abstract}
QuaRK studies frozen quantum reservoir learning for causal temporal regression
when data are dependent, measurements are finite, and only a finite pool of
random reservoir realizations can be searched.  The architecture exposes qubit
width, spatial multiplexing, and memory range, while grouped Pauli measurements
and an RMS-normalized, norm-controlled Mat\'ern readout make the remaining
statistical and measurement budgets explicit without letting feature dimension
alone distort kernel geometry.  A graph-local operator-spreading theorem
shows that product input injection cannot enlarge Pauli support, whereas a
generic cycle mixer activates higher-support components and therefore exposes
higher-order projected-input interactions directly in measured features; this
is a finite-resource representation statement, not a claim that entanglement
creates new encoding frequencies or guarantees lower risk.  We replace
validation-based reservoir ranking by full-sample empirical-risk minimization
over the finite frozen pool and give
a uniform beta-mixing bound that controls the resulting data-dependent
selection.  A width-resolved approximation theorem separates information lost
by the shared Gaussian projection from approximation by the reservoir family:
under a canonical coupled projection, the resulting population information
floor is nonincreasing with width and vanishes almost surely once the projection
is injective, while finite spatial multiplexing and finite-norm Mat\'ern
readouts introduce no additional asymptotic floor.  Continuity and full support
then give positive probability to neighborhoods of good frozen designs, so a
pool of size $S$ misses all such designs with probability exactly
$(1-\hitMass)^S$.  Combining this pool-coverage term with dependent-data
generalization, finite-shot error, and finite-window error yields an end-to-end
excess-risk certificate for the predictor actually returned.  Under a
geometric beta-mixing envelope, a raw-time gap logarithmic in sample size is
sufficient to keep the unit-block coupling term controlled.  A separate
Johnson--Lindenstrauss result is retained only as a finite-data geometry
certificate, not as a surrogate for predictive approximation.  The theory does
not claim a distribution-free numerical lower bound on $\hitMass$ or a
quantum advantage.
\end{abstract}
