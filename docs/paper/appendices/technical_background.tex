\section{Technical Background and Attribution}
\label{app:technical-background}

This appendix records the standard tools imported by the proofs and separates
them from the QuaRK-specific arguments.

\subsection{Quantum channels and norms}

An $n$-qubit state is a positive semidefinite trace-one operator on
$(\sC^2)^{\otimes n}$. The trace norm is
$\traceNorm{\mQ}=\Tr\sqrt{\mQ^\dagger\mQ}$, and the operator norm is
$\opNorm{\mQ}$. Trace duality gives
\begin{equation}
    \abs{\Tr[AB]}\leq\opNorm{A}\traceNorm{B}.
    \label{eq:trace-duality-background}
\end{equation}
The trace norm is invariant under unitary conjugation, and two density
operators are at trace distance at most two.

The map $\replacementChannel(\mQ)=\Tr[\mQ]\rhoreset$ is a replacement
channel. If $\rhoreset=\sum_j r_j\lvert j\rangle\!\langle j\rvert$ and
$\{\lvert k\rangle\}_k$ is an orthonormal basis, then
$\Kraus_{jk}=\sqrt{r_j}\lvert j\rangle\!\langle k\rvert$ satisfies
\[
    \sum_{j,k}\Kraus_{jk}\mQ\Kraus_{jk}^\dagger=\Tr[\mQ]\rhoreset,
    \qquad
    \sum_{j,k}\Kraus_{jk}^\dagger\Kraus_{jk}=\identity.
\]
Thus $\replacementChannel$ is completely positive and trace preserving by
the operator-sum representation \citep{Kraus1971}. The channel in
\Eqref{eq:reservoir-channel} is a convex combination of this replacement map
and a unitary channel.

\subsection{Gaussian finite-set embeddings}

For a fixed vector $v\neq0$ and a matrix with independent $\gN(0,1/n)$
entries, the ratio $\norm{\projection_nv}_2^2/\norm v_2^2$ is a normalized
chi-square variable. The standard Gaussian-projection tail bound used in the
proof of \cref{prop:finite-data-jl} is
\[
\Pr\!\left[
  \left|\norm{\projection_nv}_2^2-\norm v_2^2\right|
  >\epsilon\norm v_2^2
\right]
\leq
2\exp\!\left[-n\left(\frac{\epsilon^2}{4}-\frac{\epsilon^3}{6}\right)\right],
\qquad \epsilon\in(0,1).
\]
The manuscript uses this only as a finite-set statement; it does not claim
that a projection into $\sR^n$ is globally injective when $n<d$.

\subsection{Beta mixing and unit-block coupling}

Absolute regularity is monotone under sigma-algebra inclusion, and measurable
maps cannot increase it \citep{Bradley2005}. These properties yield
\Eqref{eq:augmented-window-mixing} from the raw-time gap and independent
measurement seeds.

The independent-block technique of \citet{Yu1994} compares dependent
collections with independent blocks having the same marginals. The general
Rademacher analysis of \citet{MohriRostamizadeh2008} allows arbitrary block
lengths. This paper fixes that length to one. The $N=2\mu$ windows are split
into odd and even collections of $\mu$ single-window blocks, so the coupling
cost is controlled by $\beta_{\processZ}(g)$.

For independent observations, a class bounded by $L_{\max}$ obeys
\begin{equation}
    \sup_{f\in\gF}\abs{\E f-\widehat\E f}
    \leq
    2\Rad_\mu(\gF)
    +3L_{\max}\sqrt{\frac{\log(2/\alpha)}{2\mu}}
    \label{eq:iid-rademacher-background}
\end{equation}
with probability at least $1-\alpha$ under the convention used in the
unit-block Rademacher bound. The contraction principle transfers the
prediction-class complexity to squared loss
\citep{BartlettMendelson2002}.

\subsection{Grouped Pauli measurements}
\label{app:grouped-pauli-measurements}

A global Pauli setting assigns one of $X$, $Y$, and $Z$ to each qubit. One shot
returns a sign for every qubit, and products of those signs estimate every
compatible Pauli string. Because QuaRK declares its graph-local observables in
advance, a deterministic cover can replace a generic shadow ensemble.
Overlapping tomography relates such covers to covering arrays and provides
constant-size constructions for nearest-neighbor two-body Pauli marginals
\citep{HansenneEtAl2024}.

For the cycle, a balanced nine-setting cover can be constructed explicitly.
Fix a proper vertex coloring $c:\gV_n\to\{0,1,2\}$ and identify
$\{\pauliX,\pauliY,\pauliZ\}$ with $\{0,1,2\}$. For
$(a,b)\in\{0,1,2\}^2$, define the setting whose basis at vertex $v$ is
$a+bc(v)$ modulo $3$. Every one-qubit Pauli basis then appears in exactly
three settings at each vertex. For adjacent vertices $u,v$, we have
$c(u)\neq c(v)$, so
\[
    (a,b)\longmapsto
    \bigl(a+bc(u),\,a+bc(v)\bigr)\pmod 3
\]
is a bijection of $\{0,1,2\}^2$. Hence every two-qubit Pauli pair on an edge
appears in exactly one of the nine settings. This gives $a_n(O)=3$ for
$O\in\gO_V(n)$ and $a_n(O)=1$ for $O\in\gO_E(n)$.

If $\phi=\Tr[O\rho]$, every compatible projective measurement of $O$ produces
a sign with mean $\phi$ and variance $1-\phi^2$. Pooling all $a_n(O)M$
compatible independent state preparations therefore gives coordinate
variance
\[
    \frac{1-\phi^2}{a_n(O)M}.
\]
The same shot may contribute to several compatible coordinate estimators, so
those estimators need not be independent. The finite-shot proof only sums
their coordinatewise mean-squared errors and therefore does not require such
independence.

\subsection{Expressivity and observability provenance}

The mixer analysis in \cref{sec:mixer-expressivity} uses only elementary Pauli
conjugation and real-analyticity; it does not require a capacity theorem from
the literature.  Its interpretation is informed by two complementary strands.
For rotation-based quantum models, the data-encoding generators determine the
available Fourier frequencies, whereas later circuit structure controls which
Fourier coefficients can be populated \citep{SchuldSwekeMeyer2021}.  Recent
QRC work extends this frequency/capacity viewpoint to temporal settings and
shows explicitly that increasing reservoir size or dynamical complexity need
not increase resolvable expressive capacity when input injection is the
limiting factor \citep{SchuetteEtAl2026}.

A separate line studies what the measurement interface can resolve.  The
resolvable expressive capacity/eigentask framework incorporates finite
sampling noise \citep{HuEtAl2023Eigentasks}, while Krylov observability uses
time-evolved measured operators to estimate the effective observable space and
has been shown to track QRC information-processing capacity closely
\citep{CindrakLudgeJaurigue2025,CindrakJaurigueLudge2025}.  QuaRK therefore
uses Pauli support growth and the observable-bank ablation as transparent
mechanism diagnostics.  We do not identify Pauli weight, entanglement, Krylov
dimension, or expressive capacity with predictive advantage without the
corresponding task evidence.

\subsection{Universality provenance}

Classical reservoir universality is commonly proved through fading memory,
point separation, and Stone--Weierstrass algebra arguments
\citep{GrigoryevaOrtega2018}. \citet{MonzaniPrati2025} give a unified
classical/quantum formulation and identify spatial multiplexing---parallel,
uncoupled reservoirs driven by the same input with a joint readout---as a QRC
mechanism for constructing the required polynomial algebra. We use that
established mechanism only inside the width-resolved approximation argument in
\cref{sec:width-resolved-approximation}; no novelty is claimed for the
universality principle itself.

The Mat\'ern RKHS plays a different role. Its full-support radial spectral
density makes it universal on compact Euclidean subsets
\citep{SriperumbudurEtAl2011}, while normalization gives an RKHS ball of
radius $\Lambda$ the standard Rademacher bound of order
$\Lambda/\sqrt{\mu}$.  QuaRK additionally evaluates the kernel on the RMS
feature metric $\norm{z-z'}_2/\sqrt p$.  For each finite $p$ this is simply the
invertible coordinate rescaling $z\mapsto z/\sqrt p$, so strict positive
definiteness and universality are unchanged; the section-map Lipschitz constant
with respect to the unscaled Euclidean feature vector is reduced by
$1/\sqrt p$.  We therefore do not require the fixed-radius Mat\'ern
ball itself to be a polynomial algebra. The polynomial algebra appears only
as an intermediate spatial-multiplexing construction and is approximated on
the resulting compact feature image by the Mat\'ern RKHS. This separation is
also motivated by the QRC risk analysis of \citet{ChmielewskiEtAl2025}, who
show unfavourable qubit scaling for the polynomial-readout classes they study
and discuss spatial multiplexing as an alternative algebraic mechanism.

\subsection{Novelty boundary}

The paper does not claim novelty for Gaussian random projection, replacement
channels, beta-mixing coupling, Rademacher complexity, Ivanov regularization,
Mat\'ern kernels, covering arrays, or reservoir model selection in general.
The QuaRK-specific contribution is the topology-aligned random reservoir
family, the graph-local finite-resource spreading statement for that declared
mixer/observable architecture, and explicit resource-to-risk-fidelity
guarantees under dependent temporal sampling and grouped quantum measurement.
