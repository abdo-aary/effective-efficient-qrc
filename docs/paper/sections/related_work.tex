\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related-work}

\paragraph{Reservoir computing and fading memory.}
Echo-state networks and liquid-state machines established the use of fixed
driven dynamics with trained readouts
\citep{Jaeger2001,MaassNatschlagerMarkram2002}.  Fading memory formalizes the
decreasing influence of the remote past \citep{BoydChua1985}; universal
approximation and statistical risk bounds are available for several classical
reservoir families \citep{GrigoryevaOrtega2018,GononGrigoryevaOrtega2020}.
QuaRK adopts the same separation between frozen dynamics and learned readout,
but makes the physical width, reservoir multiplicity, memory range, and
measurement budget explicit.

\paragraph{Quantum reservoirs and spatial multiplexing.}
QRC replaces the recurrent state by a driven quantum system and extracts
classical features from quantum observables \citep{FujiiNakajima2017,
ChenNurdin2019,ChenNurdinYamamoto2020}.  Existing constructions use resets,
dissipation, multiplexing, and gate-model dynamics
\citep{NakajimaEtAl2019,MujalEtAl2023,MolteniDestriPrati2023,HuEtAl2024},
while formal work studies fading-memory and universality properties of quantum
reservoir filters \citep{MartinezPenaOrtega2023,MartinezPenaOrtega2025,
MonzaniPrati2025}.  In particular, \citet{MartinezPenaOrtega2025} analyze
\emph{contracted-encoded quantum channels}: an input-encoding channel followed
by a strictly contractive channel, together with conditions under which the
resulting reservoir filter remains genuinely input-dependent.  This design
principle directly motivates the stable input-dependent channel used in QuaRK;
our contribution lies in combining it with topology-aware frozen mixing,
heterogeneous memory scales, grouped measurements, and resource-aware
learning guarantees.  We use spatial multiplexing in the standard sense of
parallel uncoupled reservoirs driven by the same input.  Its role here is
representational: finitely many separating reservoir coordinates can coexist
inside one feature map.  The learned readout remains a norm-controlled Mat\'ern
RKHS rather than the polynomial class used only in the approximation argument,
which also avoids importing the unfavorable qubit-dependent complexity of
some polynomial-readout QRC bounds \citep{ChmielewskiEtAl2025}.

\paragraph{Expressivity, operator spreading, and observability.}
Fourier analyses of parametrized quantum models show that data encoding fixes
the accessible frequency set, while the remaining circuit determines which
coefficient directions inside that set can actually be realized
\citep{SchuldSwekeMeyer2021}.  Recent QRC analysis extends this viewpoint to
temporal inputs and emphasizes that increasing reservoir size or dynamical
complexity alone need not increase resolvable expressive capacity when the
encoding is already the bottleneck \citep{SchuetteEtAl2026}.  Complementary
finite-sampling work formalizes resolvable expressive capacity and eigentasks,
including evidence that measured correlations can improve the functions
resolved above sampling noise \citep{HuEtAl2023Eigentasks}.  Krylov-based QRC
studies instead inspect the span generated by time-evolved measured operators;
Krylov observability closely tracks information-processing capacity and makes
the choice of measured observables part of the expressivity question
\citep{CindrakLudgeJaurigue2025,CindrakJaurigueLudge2025}.

QuaRK uses these results as motivation rather than importing their capacity
claims.  Our self-contained statement is narrower: product input injection
cannot enlarge Pauli support, the identity mixer therefore leaves each raw
observable coordinate confined to its seed qubits, whereas a generic
edge-local mixer activates higher-support Pauli components whose spatial reach
is controlled by graph distance.  This identifies operator spreading and
observable locality as concrete finite-resource mechanisms without claiming
that entanglement by itself improves risk or creates frequencies absent from
the encoding.

\paragraph{Random preprocessing and kernel readouts.}
Gaussian Johnson--Lindenstrauss embeddings preserve finite-set geometry at a
target dimension logarithmic in the number of points
\citep{DasguptaGupta2003,Woodruff2014}.  QuaRK uses this fact only for the raw
inputs present in the observed windows; it does not interpret finite-data
projection preservation as global injectivity.  The classical readout uses a
normalized Mat\'ern kernel evaluated on an RMS feature metric, equivalently on
features scaled by the inverse square root of their dimension.  This fixed,
label-independent rescaling removes dimension-induced distance inflation while
preserving the standard Mat\'ern function class at each finite dimension.
Mat\'ern kernels are strictly positive definite
and universal on compact Euclidean subsets under the standard spectral
conditions \citep{Genton2001,SriperumbudurEtAl2011,PorcuEtAl2024}; here those
properties provide bounded RKHS complexity and the final approximation step,
not a claim of quantum advantage.

\paragraph{Learning under temporal dependence.}
Independent-block coupling converts beta-mixing samples into nearby independent
collections and underlies Rademacher bounds for non-i.i.d. data
\citep{Yu1994,MohriRostamizadeh2008}.  Our sampling protocol fixes the
auxiliary block length to one by constructing already-gapped windows, so the
dependence term remains visible through $\beta_{\processZ}(g)$.  The resulting
uniform deviation is taken simultaneously over the finite frozen pool and the
Ivanov ball, allowing full-sample empirical-risk selection while charging the
search adaptivity explicitly through $\log S$.

\paragraph{Grouped quantum measurements.}
Large observable families can be estimated by randomized schemes such as
classical shadows \citep{HuangKuengPreskill2020}.  QuaRK instead declares a
small topology-local Pauli bank in advance and exploits a deterministic cover.
Overlapping tomography relates such schedules to covering arrays and provides
constant-size constructions for nearest-neighbor two-body Pauli marginals
\citep{HansenneEtAl2024}.  Aligning the measurement bank with the interaction
graph makes the feature dimension and state-preparation cost directly
interpretable.

The contribution is therefore not any one of these ingredients in isolation.
It is the resource-accounted combination of a frozen topology-aware QRC law,
a finite-pool learning protocol, a dependent-data finite-pool ERM certificate,
an explicit random-pool coverage term, and a width-resolved representation
argument expressed in the same
architecture/design/feature-map hierarchy.
